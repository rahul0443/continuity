# INC-VNT300-009: Uniformity drift caused by in-situ showerhead probing

**Date logged:** synthetic record
**Reported by:** Equipment Technician (synthetic)
**Severity:** Medium — 3 days of uniformity excursions before root cause found

## Symptom
Film thickness uniformity on the standard SiN monitor wafer degraded
gradually from 1.1% to over 2.0% (1-sigma) over a three-day period, exceeding
the 1.5% spec.

## Investigation
Showerhead inspection found minor blockage in the outer hole ring — within
the acceptable range per SOP-VNT300-002, but a technician on the prior shift
had used a thin probe to manually clear several of the partially blocked
holes rather than scheduling a full showerhead swap, in an attempt to avoid
tool downtime. This was not logged as a deviation from procedure at the time.

## Resolution
Probed holes showed measurable diameter distortion under microscope
inspection. Showerhead was swapped per standard procedure. Uniformity
returned to baseline (<1.2%) after the swap.

## Lesson learned
In-situ probing of blocked showerhead holes is not an acceptable workaround
even when blockage is below the swap threshold — it distorts hole geometry
in a way that is not visible during a normal inspection and produces a
uniformity drift that looks like a gradual process issue rather than an
obvious hardware failure. This is why SOP-VNT300-002 explicitly prohibits
probing; the prohibition exists because of this specific incident.
