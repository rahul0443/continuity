# INC-AMX5200-014: Scrap event from running lots through intermittent RF match fault

**Date logged:** synthetic record
**Reported by:** Shift Engineer (synthetic)
**Severity:** High — 2 lots scrapped (50 wafers)

## Symptom
RF match on Chamber 3 was tuning successfully but taking 12-14 seconds
instead of the normal <8 seconds. Reflected power briefly spiked above 5%
during tuning before settling. Because the match eventually tuned within the
"pass" criteria, the shift engineer released the chamber and ran two
production lots.

## What happened
Both lots showed elevated etch rate non-uniformity on incoming metrology,
traced back to unstable plasma ignition during the extended tuning window on
a subset of wafers early in each lot.

## Investigation
Root cause was a partially degraded match box cooling fan, which allowed the
match network electronics to run hotter than normal. This did not cause an
outright tuning failure (which would have blocked the chamber automatically)
— it only slowed and destabilized tuning, which is not the same failure
signature and was not caught by the automatic interlock.

## Resolution
Replaced match box cooling fan. Tuning time returned to <8 seconds
consistently.

## Lesson learned
A match that "eventually tunes" is not the same as a healthy match. Tuning
time and reflected-power stability during the tuning transient are leading
indicators of match box thermal issues, and should be treated as a fault
condition even when the pass/fail interlock does not trigger. This is why
SOP-AMX5200-001 requires escalation after two recurrences in a shift rather
than waiting for an outright tuning failure.
