# KI-VNT300-001: Exit Knowledge-Capture Interview — PECVD

**Interviewer:** Is there anything about the Vantage PECVD tools that you've
learned here that isn't reflected in the maintenance procedures?

**Engineer:** The seasonal humidity pattern is the big one, and it's specific
to being in Arizona, which is part of why it never came up when I was
trained on this same tool platform back home. Outside air humidity swings a
lot more here across the year than what this platform was originally
qualified against, and even with makeup air handling, we see a measurable
uptick in showerhead hole blockage rate during the monsoon season stretch,
compared to the rest of the year. It's not huge, but it's consistent — I've
watched it across two full years now.

**Interviewer:** Do you know the mechanism, or is it just a correlation
you've observed?

**Engineer:** My working theory, and I want to be clear this is a theory, not
something I've proven in a controlled way, is that ambient moisture is
affecting precursor gas line moisture content slightly upstream of the
showerhead, which changes deposition behavior right at the showerhead holes
in a way that accelerates blockage. I haven't run a formal study on it. But
practically, what it means is: the weekly PM inspection interval in
SOP-VNT300-002 is a reasonable average, but during the monsoon stretch, I'd
personally check the showerhead more like every four or five days instead of
waiting the full week, because the blockage can cross the 3% threshold faster
than the normal PM cadence catches it.

**Interviewer:** Has that ever caused a real excursion?

**Engineer:** Not a scrap event, no — we've caught it early because a couple
of us started doing the shorter interval informally during monsoon season.
But it's never been written into the procedure, so it depends entirely on
whoever happens to be on shift remembering to do it. If nobody remembers this
year, it'll just show up as an unexplained uniformity drift that someone has
to re-diagnose from scratch, probably assuming it's the susceptor heater
issue from INC-VNT300-017 first, since that's the documented uniformity
failure mode.

**Interviewer:** Anything else worth capturing before you rotate out?

**Engineer:** Just a reminder more than new information — the in-situ probing
prohibition in the SOP exists because of a real incident, not as a generic
caution. New technicians sometimes read a written rule as overly cautious
until they know the story behind it. I'd make sure whoever takes over
training understands why that rule exists, not just that it exists.
